A quick rescue guide

1. Make something in ten minutes

You can start with the included animals or the Ember dragon lesson. To make a tiny character from scratch, follow this recipe. Save a copy before experimenting.

  1. Make the body. In Blender, add a UV Sphere. Add two smaller spheres as feet and move them so they overlap the body. Keep the shapes closed, like solid lumps of clay.
  2. Join the clay. In Object Mode, select those three objects. Press N, open Meld Clay, then click Meld Selected Parts. A new clay surface surrounds your parts.
  3. Pick a finish. Open Clay Finish and choose Plasticine. Switch the viewport to Material Preview. Pick a Clay color; leave the finer controls alone for now.
  4. Shape the clay. Open Shape & Melding and choose Palm rolled. Try Thumb pressed for broad squeezes or Gently kneaded for overlapping soft pressure. Choose the finish first, then the Shape style: some finishes also set the earlier surface noise.
  5. Give it character. Click Edit Parts, select one foot, and use G to move or S to scale it. Click Finish Editing Parts when you like the silhouette.
  6. Add eyes. Open Clay Eyes, choose Round, and set Eye diameter to fit the head. Click Place Eye on Surface, point at the face, and click to place. Repeat for the other eye. Esc cancels placement.
  7. Try a soft crack. Select the body again. In Fingerprints & Cracks, choose Soft Dough, Crack width Medium, Crack displacement 0.25, Crack coverage 0.5 and Crack quality Standard. Leave density at 4.5 and scale at 1. Try the arrow beside Crack seed until you like the placement.
  8. Save and look around. Let the surface settle, or click Full Quality Now when adaptive preview is enabled. Orbit the back and underside too. Save your Blender file. Your original parts are still editable.
Presets are your starting point. Finish presets work on different Meld Clay models. Soft Dough width choices follow the model’s size; Dry Fractures width choices follow its clay-cell pattern. Tiny limbs, very thin parts and crowded details can still need a gentler width or depth. Start with Medium and Standard, then adjust only what you can see needs changing.

2. Read the sidebar

Select a clay surface to see model controls, or select a clay eye to see its eye controls. The sidebar follows that selection. Labels line up beside their fields in a wider sidebar; in a narrow sidebar, each label sits above its field. Section triangles open and close groups; this never turns an effect off.

The groups follow your workflow: Surface shape and Hand shaping; Colors and Surface texture; then Thumbprints, Crack shape, Crack layout and Crack finish. Crack tips holds extra advice. Eye shape and the three eye materials live under Clay Eyes.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Search settingsType a word such as crack, color, pupil or move. Edit matching controls directly, or click an action such as Move Whole Model. Search includes controls in closed groups. A second field in the 3D Viewport header stays available while the sidebar scrolls; its filter button opens results. Both fields share the same query. Clear it with × to return to the full sidebar.
Section triangle / More…Open a group when you want finer adjustments. Closing it keeps every value and effect. A gray control is waiting for a prerequisite; hover it or read the nearby explanation.
DropdownsClick the value to choose a named option. Custom on Crack width reveals a number you can edit while keeping the current result.
Number fieldsDrag to adjust, or click/type an exact value. Hover a control for its short explanation. Blender Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverses an edit.
Seed refresh arrowChoose another repeatable variation of that pattern. Fingerprint seed affects prints; Crack seed affects cracks; Color variation seed affects color. Random seed under Shape & Melding is the broad variation control.
Reset arrowBeside the finish, reapply its preset settings. Beside Crack color, return to automatic crack coloring. A reset arrow is different from a seed’s randomize arrow; hover to see its action.
Copy / Paste iconsCopy settings from one selected clay model and paste them onto another. The section heading tells you what will transfer. See the exact boundaries and practice below.

3. Build and edit parts

The finished clay is the visible surface. Its source parts are the simple objects underneath. Edit those parts to reshape the model.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Meld Selected PartsBuild a live clay model from selected closed mesh objects. Make adjoining parts overlap so they can blend. Separate disconnected pieces can remain separate islands.
TargetThe model that receives part actions. When adding a loose object, check this name first. Opening a model with Edit Parts establishes the intended target.
Edit Parts / Finish Editing PartsReveal the editable source cages, then return to the finished view. Move, rotate, scale or edit the sources as you normally would in Blender.
Hide CagesTemporarily hide the cage outlines for a clear look at the surface. It does not remove parts.
Add SelectedAdd selected loose mesh parts to the current target model. For example: open the body with Edit Parts, select an overlapping horn, check Target, then Add Selected.
RemoveDetach selected source parts from that model and restore their separate editable objects. It does not delete their meshes.
Recover Selected PartsRecover selected source objects left behind by a removed or broken live model. For the normal complete-model recovery action, use Restore Original Parts in Finish & Utilities.
Add Clay Critter DemoCreate a small example you can explore. Use it when you want a starting shape without building a blockout first.

4. Shape & Melding

Start with Shape style: Palm rolled for smooth clay with shallow hand pressure. Try Thumb pressed for broader compressions or Gently kneaded for a more uneven handmade silhouette. Keep Surface cleanup Auto for small remeshing steps. The recipes set Knead / soften 45. Pressure follows the size of each source piece.

Existing models open unchanged in Custom. Choosing a named style explicitly adopts the new shaping, retaining editable sources and recovery backups. Choose Enable Clay Shaping to enable individual controls with no added rounding or pressure. Hold Shape still protects individual parts. If your old ball stays faceted and its model has one source part, select the finished ball and lower Hold Shape directly below Knead / soften. Set it to 0 to allow full artistic rounding. Searching hold exposes the same slider. For models with several parts, use Edit Parts, select the source you want to adjust, and change Hold Shape in Join Controls. Finish Editing Parts returns to the surface.

Older softening graphs may also offer Update Softening. Save a working copy before comparing new shaping with an authored model. Rounding changes the surface that joins, cracks and bites follow, so inspect those features after a shape change.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Shape stylePalm rolled: nearly round, shallow pressure. Thumb pressed: fewer, broader compressions. Gently kneaded: overlapping soft squeezes. Custom keeps the current result. Changing a recipe setting switches to Custom without resetting the other values. Some Clay Finish presets also set the earlier surface noise, which can make the style read Custom while preserving Source rounding and Hand pressure. Pick your finish first, then a Shape style when you want its complete recipe. New models start at Palm rolled.
Enable Clay ShapingEnable the live source-rounding and hand-pressure controls on an existing model, starting at zero added effect. Preserve the saved shape and retain previous geometry, material and crack-capture data as recovery backups.
Source roundingHow strongly the coarse editable source is reconstructed as a curved surface before melding. Knead scales this effect up to 45. Hold Shape reduces it on protected parts. Zero keeps the source contours.
Hand pressureSmooth broad squeezes, measured as a percentage of each source piece radius. This shapes the geometry; it does not add grain or fingerprints. Zero adds no pressure. Start with a named style instead of guessing a number.
Pressure areasHow many broad squeeze areas surround each source piece. The recipes use three, two and five respectively. More areas do not add extra material texture.
Pressure breadthHow widely each squeeze spreads. Larger values produce broader, gentler pressure areas. Use Random seed for another arrangement.
Live surfaceKeep the clay updating while you shape its parts. Pause it temporarily when arranging a complex blockout; turn it back on to see the result.
ResolutionHow finely the base clay surface is built. Start at 128; try 72 for a draft or 192 for smaller details. More resolution costs memory and time.
Surface cleanupAuto gently reduces small steps left by rebuilding the surface, even on held parts at Knead / soften 0. Off skips that cleanup. Off together with Knead / soften 0 skips cleanup and kneading. Independent Hand pressure and surface-noise settings can still change the form. Auto is a small cleanup pass; it cannot erase every flat face in a coarse source shape.
Knead / softenRound corners and soften intersections. With new shaping enabled, 0 also disables source rounding, while 45 gives the full chosen Source rounding. Higher values continue softening the melded surface. Hand pressure and surface noise remain independent. Lower Knead or use Hold Shape on individual source parts to protect small contours. Try 0 → 45 → 120 → 45, then inspect the silhouette, seams and thin tips.
Hold ShapeShown here when the model has one source part. Protect that part from artistic rounding and hand pressure; lower it for softer contours, or set it to 0 to remove that protection. This edits the source’s existing value, also available by searching hold. Installation and shape presets keep your saved Hold value. For a model with several parts, select a source through Edit Parts and use Hold Shape in Join Controls.
Update SofteningOffered on existing models that use an earlier method, including the first Surface cleanup build. Adopt the current cleanup and kneading on this model while retaining your knob values, editable sources and recovery backups. New models already use the current method. This is separate from Update Form Variation or the crack update actions.
Inflate / deflateMake source pieces slightly fuller or smaller before they join. A small positive amount can close a small contact gap.
MidlevelA finer adjustment to the finished surface’s fullness. 0.5 is neutral; lower inflates and higher deflates. Leave it at 0.5 unless you need that adjustment.
More shape controlsOpen the earlier proportion and surface-noise controls. Closing this group keeps their effects. Named shape recipes set Form variation and Hand-shaped amount to zero, using broad Hand pressure instead.
Form variationThe earlier seeded axis-proportion control, kept for saved looks and individual adjustments. 0 restores the original part proportions. It is not an attachment-aware character proportion system.
Hand-shaped amountThe earlier normal-direction surface noise, applied after softening. It remains available for rougher looks. For broad playdough squeezes, use Hand pressure or a Shape style.
Hand-shaped scaleChange the size of that unevenness. Lower values make broader features; higher values make smaller, more frequent features.
Random seed + arrowChange the repeatable variation shared by shape and surface patterns. Use the separate print, color or crack seed when you want to change only that feature.

5. Work comfortably, then check the finish

Preview controls help when dragging parts becomes slow. Let the model return to full quality before judging seams, crack walls or color.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Adaptive previewTemporarily use a lighter surface while editing, then return to the selected full quality after you pause.
Use Faster PreviewEnable a practical moving preview configuration. Inspect the optional settings afterward if you want a different speed/detail balance.
Moving qualityHow much detail to keep while moving. Lower values are lighter and can look simpler during the drag.
Refine afterHow long to wait after editing pauses before restoring full quality.
Fast fusion while movingUse a simpler temporary join calculation during motion. The settled result returns to the normal join rules.
Light material while movingTemporarily simplify fine shader details while moving. Colors remain useful for editing; wait for full quality to judge grain, prints and crack shading.
Full Quality NowFinish the current refinement immediately. Use before close inspection or comparing screenshots.

6. Join Controls

Start with a pair of overlapping source parts. A join rule controls the contact between that pair; it is separate from the all-over crack effect.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Selected partsShows the two source parts your next join action will use. Select exactly two cages after Edit Parts.
Create Selected Join / Edit Selected JoinCreate a rule for the selected pair, or open its existing rule. Pick Blended or Pressed, then adjust the active controls.
Saved join rulesA list of existing rules. Read the listed pair’s names before editing; the list and your currently selected pair can differ.
Edit Listed JoinSwitch to the pair in the saved list and edit that rule. The different-pair message warns when this changes your selection.
Select Join PartsSelect the two source parts belonging to the listed rule.
Blended / PressedBlended uses the normal soft joining. Pressed keeps a defined contact with a recessed seam and enables the crease controls.
Crease strengthHow strongly to press the seam into the contact.
Crease widthHow broad an area around the contact the seam affects. It follows the smaller part’s size.
End taperEase exposed seam ends into the surface. 0 keeps the full seam. A closed contact loop has no exposed ends to taper.
Hold ShapeHelp one selected source part retain its form. Use Edit Parts and select a source to adjust it here; it belongs to that part, so adjust each haunch or limb separately. For a model with only one source part, the same value is directly available in Shape & Melding and search without entering Edit Parts. It protects the part from artistic rounding and hand pressure. Auto cleanup still reduces small remeshing steps on held parts, including at Knead / soften 0.
Copy Settings / Paste SettingsTransfer join style, strength, width and End taper. Hold Shape is not copied; set it on the destination part.
Remove Join RuleRemove that pair’s special rule and return its contact to the normal melding behavior. The source parts remain.

8. Clay Finish

Choose a finish first, then customize its colors and texture. Finish presets also carry their intended crack and surface settings, so changing the preset can change more than the color.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Finish dropdownChoose a prepared material. Try Plasticine for a simple clay character, Terracotta for a warm earthy surface, Bubble Gum for a glossy toy or Dry Cracked Earth for clay cells.
Reset Finish arrowReapply the chosen finish’s settings. Enable Keep my colors first if you want to retain your palette.
Keep my colorsPreserve Clay color and Second clay color when choosing or resetting a finish.
Clay colorThe main surface color. Use Material Preview or Rendered view to judge it.
Color mixingMix in a second clay color. Values above zero reveal Second clay color and Color patch scale.
Second clay colorThe other color used by the mixture.
Color patch scaleChange how large or frequent the mixed patches appear. Adjust while looking at the model.
RoughnessLow values are shinier; high values are more matte.
Color variationAdd uneven color within the finish.

Surface grainAmount of fine surface texture. It affects how light catches the material, without adding mesh geometry.
Grain frequencyHigher values make finer, more frequent grain. It becomes relevant when Surface grain is above zero.
Mineral flecksAdd small speckled inclusions. Available in the finish controls even when the selected preset starts without flecks.

9. Fingerprints & Cracks: thumbprints

Thumbprints are material relief: they catch the light like pressed skin ridges, while the clay mesh remains unchanged. Try a close view in Material Preview.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Thumbprint sizeResize impressions around their existing centers. 1 is their normal size.
Finger impressionsStrength of the thumbprint relief. 0 turns it off.
Print densitySpacing of the scattered prints. Higher values generally pack them closer together.
Thumbprint stretch1 makes rounder pressure patches. Higher values make longer, narrower impressions with a similar area.
Print irregularityGently bend and vary the ridge paths inside each impression.
Fingerprint seed + arrowGenerate another arrangement of print locations, angles and ridge families. Color, cracks and mesh shape stay unchanged.

10. Fingerprints & Cracks: cracks

Start with a style, Medium width and Standard quality. Then choose the seed and depth you like. Change one control at a time. Width edits do not need a new seed.

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Crack styleSoft Dough: flowing grooves, fuller middles and pinched tips. Dry Fractures: a connected network of clay-cell boundaries. Either can accompany any clay finish.
Crack displacementPhysical recessed depth, scaled to the model size. Start around 0.25–0.50. 0 removes mesh depth. Deeper, narrower cracks ask more of the available geometry.
Crack width: Fine / Medium / WideNamed width choices. Soft Dough follows model size; Dry Fractures follows its cell pattern. New ordinary models start at Medium. Authored finish presets and existing models may have their own custom widths.
Crack width: CustomKeep the current opening and reveal its editable value. Selecting Custom alone changes no crack shape or seed.
Opening / Opening (local)Soft Dough Custom: target full width at the widest middle; the ends taper. Uses scene units or BU (Blender units). Uniform scaling is included. Uneven scaling shows Opening (local), because the visible width then depends on direction.
Width factorDry Fractures Custom: 1.0 is Medium, 0.5 Fine and 2.0 Wide. Actual gaps vary along the cells. Larger cells give larger gaps at the same factor. Dry Cracked Earth starts at its authored 1.5 factor.
Crack coverageHow much of the crack pattern remains. Lower it for fewer or shorter grooves; 0 removes the main crack pattern.
Crack seed + arrowChoose another repeatable crack layout without changing the body or thumbprints. Inspect several views before choosing one.
Crack detailMake the paths wander more or less.
Layout densityRearrange pattern spacing while retaining the seed. Higher values generally pack more cracks into the surface; this is not simply adding cracks between fixed existing ones. Soft Dough retains the chosen opening, and crowded paths may shorten or disappear.
Crack scaleSoft Dough: shorten or extend grooves along their paths. Dry Fractures: resize the connected clay-cell network. Use width for openings rather than using scale as a width control.
Fine fissures / Glaze crackleDry Fractures: add smaller material-only creases. Lower values remove some branches while the remaining ones keep their strength. Raku with Soft Dough: Glaze crackle adds hairline crazing. Ordinary Soft Dough has no Fine fissures control.
Crack color + reset arrowTint recessed cracks. Initially the color follows a darker shade of the clay; editing the swatch chooses a custom color. Reset restores automatic color.
Crack quality: Base meshA light working view or lower-poly bake using the existing surface geometry. Walls and color can look approximate when there are too few faces around the opening.
Crack quality: StandardThe normal starting choice for finished renders. Adds more geometry near the crack walls within a limited budget.
Crack quality: Close-upAllow more geometry for demanding cracks and closer cameras. It costs more processing and memory.
Refinement/status messagesExplain when refinement is running, limited by its budget or ready. These are information, not buttons. Wait after a settings edit before judging the settled walls.
If a wall looks rough: let refinement finish, try Close-up, increase Resolution, use fewer cracks, widen the opening a little or lower depth. Dense, narrow and deep cracks all compete for the same available faces. Standard is a useful starting point, not a promise that every possible setting will fit every shape.

13. Copy and paste without surprises

Use the Copy icon on a section heading, select the destination clay model, then use that section’s Paste icon. One paste is one action you can Undo. Copying changes nothing on the source.

  1. On the first model, choose a finish and customize the Crack width, color and seed.
  2. Copy Clay Finish. Select a second live clay model and paste Clay Finish once.
  3. Compare the palette, prints and crack choices. The settings transfer; their arrangement naturally follows the second model’s shape and size.
  4. Undo, then Redo. Next copy only Fingerprints & Cracks onto a model with a different Clay color. Its base palette should stay intact.
ControlWhat it does / when to use it
Clay Finish Copy / PasteTransfers the complete finish, including its colors, print controls, crack controls, crack color mode, seeds and optical settings. It does not copy source parts, join rules, movement mode or a custom material override.
Fingerprints & Cracks Copy / PasteTransfers prints and cracks, including both style widths, quality and color mode. It leaves the destination’s base Clay color, finish preset and shape settings intact.
Shape & Melding Copy / PasteTransfers the Shape style selection, Source rounding, Hand pressure, Pressure areas, Pressure breadth, other shape settings, Random seed, Surface cleanup and adaptive-preview preferences. Source meshes, their placement, per-part Hold Shape, join rules and materials stay on the destination. Copying current shaping to an older destination may require Update Softening and Enable Clay Shaping first; follow the message, then paste again. A copy from before the shaping controls existed sets new rounding and pressure to 0 and the style to Custom, retaining the destination’s shaping method. A pre-cleanup copy keeps the destination’s cleanup choice; an earlier Auto/Off copy transfers that choice. Paste does not silently upgrade the destination’s algorithms.
Join Copy Settings / Paste SettingsTransfers join style, strength, width and End taper. It does not copy the selected parts or their Hold Shape amounts.
Clipboard lifetimeThese are Meld Clay settings clipboards for the current Blender session, not a saved preset library. Copy again after restarting Blender. A Paste action is unavailable until that section has been copied.
Different model sizesSoft Dough copies a width relative to the model, so the destination can show a different Opening measurement. Dry width follows its cell pattern. Matching settings do not mean identical crack placement on two different shapes.

Eyes have their own controls. Model section Copy/Paste does not edit an eye. To reuse a customized eye, keep it selected when using Add Eye at Cursor or Place Eye on Surface; a new eye starts from that eye’s settings. Reposition Selected Eye moves the existing eye.

16. A quick rescue guide

ControlWhat it does / when to use it
I cannot see my finishSwitch to Material Preview or Rendered view. Solid/MatCap is useful for shape checks but does not show the finished procedural material.
I cannot find a controlSearch its name at the top. Select the model or eye you intend to edit, open the relevant More… group, and check any prerequisite message.
A horn disappeared or a seam will not blendCheck the intended target and source collection. Ensure closed parts overlap enough. Raise Resolution for a feature too thin to survive melding; reduce Knead / soften or increase that part’s Hold Shape if rounding removes its character. Auto cleanup cannot restore missing source detail.
Fingers or knuckles lose their definitionLower Knead / soften, or use Edit Parts, select the finger or knuckle source, and increase its Hold Shape under Join Controls. Compare gradually rather than assuming the highest setting is best. Auto cleanup still cleans small remeshing steps while Hold Shape protects the intended form. Keeping the overall hand full does not guarantee that its individual finger contours survive global rounding.
The crack is too wideChoose Medium or Fine first. Custom lets you inspect the current value without changing it. With Dry Fractures, also check the cell size; with Soft Dough, density does not secretly widen the chosen opening.
Crack walls or fill look steppedWait for refinement, select Standard or Close-up, then consider more Resolution, less depth, fewer cracks or a slightly wider opening.
A preset changed my favorite paletteUndo, enable Keep my colors, then choose the preset again. To reuse just cracks and prints, copy Fingerprints & Cracks instead of Clay Finish.
The viewport is slowUse Faster Preview while editing; inspect the settled full-quality result afterward. Heavy models and dense details still take time.
I need to share a problemKeep a copy of the file. Include the selected object, Blender version, exact settings and a screenshot. Open the support page.
